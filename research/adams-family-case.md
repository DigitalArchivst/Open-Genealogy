# From John Adams Morgan back to President John Adams

**Who was the most recently deceased direct descendant we could document?** In this search, John Adams Morgan, the Olympic sailor, died on January 23, 2025. His memorial and Olympedia agree on his birth and death dates. His Adams ancestry runs through his mother. This seven-person case ends with the second U.S. president. [Morgan memorial, biographical paragraph](https://everloved.com/life-of/john-adams-morgan/obituary/) · [Olympedia, John Morgan](https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/63058)

## Work backward, one parent at a time

Start with the death record. At each step, look for a source that names the parent. Check a second account and use dates, spouses or family group numbers to distinguish people with the same name. The descriptions below are research abstracts. Follow the citations to see the source wording.

### 1. John Adams Morgan (1930–2025) → Catherine (Adams) Morgan (1902–1988)

Morgan’s memorial identifies his mother as Catherine Lovering Adams Morgan. The numbered Pierpont genealogy places John, born September 17, 1930, under Catherine Adams and Henry Sturgis Morgan. A 2011 notice for his brother Henry identifies their parents and includes John A. Morgan among his brothers. These are separately published accounts. The underlying family informants may overlap. [Morgan memorial, biographical paragraph](https://everloved.com/life-of/john-adams-morgan/obituary/) · [Pierpont genealogies, family 162323124, child 1623231244](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/rak/gen/pier/piergenn.htm) · [2011 notice for Henry Sturgis Morgan Jr.](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/legacyremembers/henry-morgan-obituary?id=20493092)

### 2. Catherine (Adams) Morgan (1902–1988) → Charles Francis Adams III (1866–1954)

Bartlett’s 1927 genealogy, family 23, p. 147, lists Catherine as Charles’s daughter and identifies her 1923 marriage to Henry Sturgis Morgan. The catalogue for Morgan’s 1932 portrait identifies his wife as the daughter of the Navy secretary. Her married surname connects the two generations. [J. Gardner Bartlett, Henry Adams of Somersetshire, England, and Braintree, Mass. (1927), families 18 and 23](https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G000015.pdf) · [Morgan portrait catalogue, biographical note for the 1932 sitting](https://english.jqadams.art/werke/henry-morgan-1932)

The Harvard Class of 1888 records provide a check made during the father’s lifetime. The sixth report lists his daughter Catherine, born January 13, 1902. The eighth lists both Catherine and his parents. The ninth agrees about the relationships but prints 1903 for Catherine. The earlier reports and Bartlett favor 1902. These successive editions belong to one series. [Sixth report, p. 15](https://archive.org/details/n6secretarysreport1888harvuoft/page/n20/mode/1up) · [Eighth report, Charles Francis Adams entry](https://archive.org/details/n8secretarysrepo1888harvuoft/page/n14/mode/1up) · [Ninth report, p. 1](https://archive.org/details/secretarysreport1888harvuoft/page/n14/mode/1up)

### 3. Charles Francis Adams III (1866–1954) → John Quincy Adams (1833–1894)

The Navy biography names the secretary’s father John Quincy Adams. Bartlett’s family 23 connects the man born in Quincy on August 2, 1866, to family 18, the John Quincy Adams born in 1833. The Massachusetts Historical Commission also identifies the relationship and distinguishes the secretary’s uncle Charles Francis Adams Jr. The suffix III alone would give the wrong father. [Naval History and Heritage Command, Charles Francis Adams III](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/biographical-files/modern-biographical-files-ndl/modern-bios-a/adams-charles-francis-iii.html) · [J. Gardner Bartlett, Henry Adams of Somersetshire, England, and Braintree, Mass. (1927), families 18 and 23](https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G000015.pdf) · [Massachusetts Historical Commission, Concord property inventory, historical narrative](https://wpadmin.sunbeamtv.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2019/12/concord_415.pdf)

Harvard’s class record names both of the secretary’s parents, John Quincy Adams and Fanny Crowninshield. This resolves a generational error in the Miller Center biography, which calls him a grandson of the sixth president. His grandfather was the diplomat Charles Francis Adams (1807–1886). The sixth president was his great-grandfather. [Class record with both parents](https://archive.org/details/n8secretarysrepo1888harvuoft/page/n14/mode/1up) · [Miller Center biography](https://millercenter.org/president/hoover/essays/adams-1929-secretary-of-the-navy)

### 4. John Quincy Adams (1833–1894) → Charles Francis Adams (1807–1886)

Bartlett’s family 18 names Charles Francis as father. Figure 1.1 in Ziolkowski lists the younger John Quincy among the children of Charles Francis Adams and Abigail Brown Brooks. The older president also recorded his son Charles announcing a son in September 1833. That contemporary entry corroborates the birth but leaves the baby unnamed. [J. Gardner Bartlett, Henry Adams of Somersetshire, England, and Braintree, Mass. (1927), families 18 and 23](https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G000015.pdf) · [Jan M. Ziolkowski, The Juggler of Notre Dame, vol. 3, figure 1.1](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/45/29/72/2019452972/2019452972.pdf) · [John Quincy Adams diary, September 23, 1833](https://www.primarysourcecoop.org/publications/jqa/document/jqadiaries-v23-1833-09-p507--entry23)

### 5. Charles Francis Adams (1807–1886) → President John Quincy Adams (1767–1848)

In his September 13, 1807 diary entry the father connects his child born on the eighteenth of the preceding month to the baptismal name Charles-Francis. The House biography also names his father and gives August 18 as Charles’s birth date. Its source for parentage is unspecified. [John Quincy Adams diary, September 13, 1807](https://www.primarysourcecoop.org/publications/jqa/document/jqadiaries-v27-1807-09-p305--entry13) · [U.S. House biography of Charles Francis Adams](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/8298)

### 6. President John Quincy Adams (1767–1848) → President John Adams (1735–1826)

In his autobiography John Adams names John Quincy Adams as his son and gives his 1767 birth in Braintree. The National Archives description of the Adams Family Papers explicitly calls the sixth president the son of John Adams. This distinguishes the president from his grandson of the same name born in 1833. [John Adams, autobiography, Works, volume 2](https://oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/oll3/store/titles/2100/Adams_1431-02_EBk_v6.0.pdf) · [National Archives, Adams Family Papers](https://www.archives.gov/nhprc/projects/catalog/adams-papers)

## How far the evidence reaches

Public death notices, contemporary interviews, compiled genealogies and a dated parent-link search in Wikidata were checked through November 27, 2026. The search included Adams and Morgan names and branches with changed surnames. WikiTree’s public descendant search returned 134 profiles across up to ten generations and no recorded death from January 23, 2025 through the cutoff. Wikidata returned Morgan for that interval. These indexes cover different people. The linked records support all six parent-child relationships. They cannot establish the latest death across every descendant of John Adams. Private notices, unindexed records and branches missing from public trees can change the answer.

An August 2026 notice for Robert E. Adams describes ancestry in the family line that also produced President Adams. It gives no chain from the president to Robert. A later death date qualifies only when direct parentage can be established. [August 2026 notice for Robert E. Adams](https://www.flannerbuchanan.com/obituaries/robert-adams)
